    Frequently asked questions // Mastodon user stats

    How many users does Mastodon have?▼

    As of Oct 11, 2026 (UTC), joinmastodon.org’s statistics counted 10,503,903 accounts on the servers that report to it. joinmastodon.org counted 7,384 servers on Oct 11, 2026. The figure is the sum over all reporting servers, so it can fall when a server leaves the count or stops reporting, and it includes accounts that were never active. FediDB crawls Mastodon-software servers on its own and counted 8.82M accounts in its reading for Oct 11, 2026 (taken 2026-10-12 05:17 UTC); the two totals are different bases and are never mixed on this page.

    How many monthly active users does Mastodon have?▼

    728,997 accounts were active in the 30 days to Oct 11, 2026, which is 6.94% of all counted accounts. Monthly active users (MAU) is a trailing 30-day window, not a daily count, so it moves slowly. The record is 2.62M on Nov 30, 2022, at the height of the Twitter migration.

    How fast is Mastodon growing?▼

    Over the last 7 days total users changed by an average of +3.5K/day and monthly active users by +272/day. The latest day was +2.8K for total users and +792 for MAU. These are net changes, not registrations: deleted accounts and servers that stop reporting are subtracted. The largest single usable day in this data is +135.5K on Nov 7, 2022; days that jump by a step no real event explains are flagged and already left out of that peak (see below).

    How many servers and posts per day does Mastodon have?▼

    joinmastodon.org counted 7,384 servers on Oct 11, 2026. Posts per day come from FediDB’s running count of statuses on Mastodon-software servers: this page stores one cumulative reading per day, taken when the update runs, and shows the change between readings divided by the time between them. The first figure appears once two daily readings exist. FediDB counts Mastodon software only, so its numbers are not comparable with the joinmastodon user counts.

    Where does this Mastodon data come from?▼

    Total users, monthly active users and server counts come from joinmastodon.org’s public statistics API (api.joinmastodon.org/statistics). That API only returns the most recent 30 days or so, so a GitHub Action reads it twice a day and keeps the history in this repository, which goes back to Sep 30, 2022. Posts per day and the FediDB totals come from FediDB (api.fedidb.org), read by the same Action. When the page loads, your browser also asks the statistics API for the current figure; that is what LIVE means.

    Why are some days missing or flagged?▼

    Some days have no reading in the archive. Those days are drawn as gaps and nothing is interpolated. Days where the source jumped by a step that reversed within days, or that no real event explains, are flagged: a flagged value is treated as missing and left out of averages, peaks, comparisons and ratios. Flagged days appear as shaded gaps in the charts and as “flagged” in the data table. Real surges such as the Nov 2022 Twitter wave are not flagged.

    Where do the leaderboards, top posts and decentralization figures come from?▼

    Server boards and the decentralization meter come from FediDB’s list of Mastodon servers, ranked by users. Account boards and top posts are built daily from the public directories and trending posts of the largest servers, so they are not an exhaustive list of Mastodon accounts or posts. Only public, discoverable accounts with at least 10,000 followers are named (no bots, locked, group or opted-out accounts), and a top post must be public, not marked sensitive and carry no content warning. Everyone else still counts toward the totals but is never listed.

    Definitions & caveats

    • Total users is joinmastodon.org’s user_count: accounts on all servers that report to its statistics. It is not registrations: it falls when a server stops reporting or accounts are removed, and it includes accounts that never became active. Each day’s value is the figure the API gives for that UTC day.
    • Monthly active users (MAU) is active_user_count: accounts active in the trailing 30 days. Because the window rolls, a day’s change is the activity of the day that entered minus the day that left, not new activity. It is not a daily active count.
    • MAU health and net signups are the day-over-day change of MAU and of total users. Both are net: sign-ups minus deletions and servers leaving the count. MAU health is the primary view because total users also moves when servers join or leave. Growth % is the change as a share of the previous day’s value, for the metric you picked last.
    • Servers is server_count from the same API. The archive holds it only for the days the updater (or a backfill) could read it, so its history is shorter than the user counts. The server series starts on Dec 1, 2022.
    • Posts per day is a rate from FediDB’s cumulative status counter for Mastodon software. The archive keeps one reading per day, taken when the update runs (the time is not fixed), with its timestamp. A day’s figure is the change since the previous reading divided by the time between the two readings, so uneven gaps do not distort it. It is left out when a reading is missing or flagged, when the readings are less than 6 hours apart, or when the counter moved backwards.
    • Ratios: MAU ÷ total users (engagement); posts per MAU divides FediDB posts by joinmastodon MAU, two sources, so read its trend, not its level; users per server divides total users by servers. FediDB totals (servers, posts, users) cover Mastodon-software servers only and are a different base from joinmastodon’s: they are labelled and never mixed into the joinmastodon series.
    • Flagged and missing days: a flagged value is treated as missing everywhere (gap, averages, net changes, peaks, ATH, comparisons, ratios). A day without a row is also a gap; nothing is interpolated.
    • Leaderboards (LDR), top posts (PST) and decentralization (DEC): server boards use FediDB’s server list; account boards use server directories and trending posts of the largest servers and list only public, discoverable accounts with 10,000+ followers. Gainers and losers are the net change since an earlier daily snapshot, so they need history. The list is a sample of the network, not a ranking of every account.
    • Trend badges compare 7-day averages at the start and end of the selected range (1W = week over week, 1M = month over month, 1Y = year over year). COMPARE overlays the same number of calendar days immediately before the range.
    • Not available: Mastodon has no public per-day counts of likers, posters or blockers and no global account index, so this page has no daily active users, posters or block leaderboards like its Bluesky sibling.
    • Surge windows are editorial. The Nov 2022 and Jul 2023 chips show the net change in total users the data records for the window, flagged steps excluded. The Feb 2024 and Sep 2024 windows each hold a one-day step in joinmastodon’s crawler sum that was reversed later while monthly active users did not follow it: they are flagged as artifacts, not sign-ups, so their chips say FLAGGED (hover a chip for the raw figures).

    STU Studies & modes

    • 7D MA: a trailing 7-calendar-day average. Missing and flagged days are skipped (a window needs at least 4 good days), so gaps never pull the average toward zero. With MA on, daily lines turn faint and the average is drawn solid.
    • LOG: a logarithmic y-axis for MAU, SRV, REC and TOT. The velocity panel stays linear because net changes can be negative.
    • MAU HEALTH · SIGNUPS · GROWTH % (USR): the net daily change of monthly active users, the net daily change of total users, or the percentage growth of whichever of the two you picked last.
    • SERVERS · USERS/SERVER (SRV) and MAU/TOTAL · POSTS/MAU · USERS/SERVER (RAT) switch what those panels plot.
    • Surge windows: one click filters every panel to a historical window and shows signups; the chip shows the net change in total users the data records there, flagged steps excluded. Click it again, press ✕ RESET or pick any range to return to your previous view. The labels are editorial. The two 2024 windows are flagged crawler steps, not sign-ups: open them to see the shaded gaps.
    • Zoom: Ctrl + scroll or pinch to zoom, drag to pan, ⟲ to reset. PNG and CSV exports cover the visible window.

    DEF Definitions

    • Total users: accounts on all servers that report to joinmastodon.org’s statistics. Net, not registrations: it can fall when servers leave or accounts are removed.
    • Monthly active users: accounts active in the trailing 30 days, as joinmastodon.org reports it. A rolling window, not a daily count.
    • Net signups / MAU health: today’s value minus yesterday’s, for total users and for monthly active users.
    • Servers: servers reporting to joinmastodon.org. Posts per day: the change between two readings of FediDB’s status counter divided by the time between them (Mastodon software only).
    • FediDB figures are a different base from joinmastodon’s and are always labelled; there are no per-day likers, posters or blockers on Mastodon.

    LDR Leaderboards, top posts & decentralization

    • LDR <GO>: top 25 servers (largest, most active, gainers and losers) or accounts (most followed, gainers and losers, most active). Pick SERVERS or ACCOUNTS, then a board and a window (24H, 7D). Gainers and losers are net changes since an earlier daily snapshot and stay empty until two snapshots that far apart exist. An account row shows followers, posts, last post, account age and its server.
    • PST <GO>: the 10 most-favourited posts among the trending candidates of the scanned servers, created on the reported UTC day, with favourites, boosts and replies. These are trending candidates, not all posts.
    • DEC <GO>: how concentrated Mastodon users are: the share held by the largest server, the top 5 and the top 10, and how many servers hold half of all users. Base: FediDB, Mastodon servers.
    • Sources: FediDB (server list and totals) and each server’s public Mastodon API (directories and trending posts). The account list is not exhaustive: accounts are found through server directories and trending posts.
    • Guardrails: only public, discoverable accounts with at least 10,000 followers are named; bots, locked, group, opted-out and moved accounts are left out. A top post must be public, not sensitive and without a content warning. Everyone else still counts toward the totals.
